SOURCE NOTE
The uploaded PDF is a Bookey educational summary of John J. Murphy's
"Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets," not the complete original
book. These notes therefore summarize what is actually present in the
uploaded material. They do not claim to reproduce the complete original

PURPOSE
Keep the important technical-analysis concepts in a form that is easy to
study, revise, and later combine with the other trading books.

============================================================
1. CORE IDEA OF TECHNICAL ANALYSIS
============================================================

The source defines technical analysis as the study of market action through
charting in order to forecast future trends.

The main market-action inputs discussed are:
- Price
- Volume
- Open interest

TECHNICAL VS FUNDAMENTAL ANALYSIS

Technical analysis:
- Concentrates on price and other market-action data.
- Uses charts and technical indicators.
- Attempts to identify trends, patterns, momentum, support/resistance,
  and timing.

Fundamental analysis:
- Focuses on economic conditions, company performance, supply and demand,
  and other underlying factors.

The source presents technical analysis as primarily concerned with what
the market is doing, while fundamental analysis examines factors that
may drive those market conditions.

============================================================
2. THE THREE BASIC PREMISES EMPHASIZED
============================================================

The source highlights three central technical-analysis ideas:

1. MARKET ACTION DISCOUNTS EVERYTHING
The market price is treated as reflecting the information and expectations
that are already influencing participants.

2. PRICES MOVE IN TRENDS
Markets can develop directional movements rather than moving completely
randomly at every moment.

3. HISTORY TENDS TO REPEAT ITSELF
Recurring market behavior and patterns can be studied because market
participants can respond in similar ways under similar conditions.

IMPORTANT:
These are premises of the technical-analysis framework presented by the
source. They are not guarantees that every pattern will work.

============================================================
3. DOW THEORY
============================================================

The source presents Dow Theory as a foundation of classical technical
analysis.

THREE TREND CLASSIFICATIONS

A. PRIMARY TREND
The major or long-term direction of the market.

B. SECONDARY / INTERMEDIATE TREND
A movement against or within the primary trend that lasts for a shorter
period.

C. MINOR / NEAR-TERM TREND
Short-term price movement.

A useful hierarchy is therefore:

PRIMARY TREND
    -> SECONDARY / INTERMEDIATE MOVES
        -> MINOR / NEAR-TERM MOVES

CONFIRMATION

The source emphasizes confirmation between market averages in Dow Theory.
The basic idea is that one average should not be considered a complete
signal by itself when confirmation from another related average is
expected.

The source specifically notes that either the Industrial or Rail average
may provide the signal and that confirmation is used to strengthen the
trend interpretation.

PRACTICAL LESSON
When studying a market, do not interpret one piece of price action without
considering the broader market trend and confirming evidence.

============================================================
4. RANDOM WALK THEORY VS TECHNICAL ANALYSIS
============================================================

The source contrasts the technical-analysis approach with Random Walk
Theory.

RANDOM WALK VIEW:
- Price changes are serially independent.
- Past price movements cannot reliably predict future price movements.

TECHNICAL-ANALYSIS VIEW:
- Market action contains observable patterns.
- Trends can be identified.
- Historical price behavior can provide information for analysis.

The PDF presents these as contrasting frameworks. It does not establish
through this summary alone that one theory is universally correct.

============================================================
5. IMPORTANT BASIC TERMS
============================================================

ACCUMULATION
The source describes accumulation as informed buying by astute investors.

DISTRIBUTION
Informed selling.

CONFIRMATION
Additional evidence supporting a price/trend signal.

DIVERGENCE
A secondary indicator fails to confirm what price is doing.

CORRECTION / RETRACEMENT
A temporary movement against the prevailing trend rather than necessarily
a complete reversal.

FAILURE SWING
A reversal pattern in which price fails to achieve a prior high/low in the
expected way, suggesting that the existing trend may be weakening.

TREND
A pattern of rising or falling peaks and troughs.

MARKET ACTION
Price, volume, and open interest.

TIMING
Selecting when to enter or exit rather than merely deciding the broad
market direction.

============================================================
6. CHART TYPES
============================================================

The source covers three major classical chart types and candlesticks.

A. BAR CHART
Shows:
- Open
- High
- Low
- Close

A vertical line represents the high-to-low range.
Horizontal marks indicate opening and closing prices.

B. LINE CHART
Connects closing prices over time.

Advantages:
- Simple
- Easy to see the general trend

Limitation:
- Omits the detailed open/high/low information of a bar chart.

C. POINT AND FIGURE CHART
Focuses on price movement rather than elapsed time.
It uses X and O columns.

Key use emphasized later:
- Identifying support/resistance zones
- Producing defined entry/exit points
- Reducing distraction from minor time-based fluctuations

D. CANDLESTICK CHART
Shows:
- Open
- High
- Low
- Close

The source calls the central open-to-close portion the real body.
The upper and lower extremes are represented by shadows/wicks.

============================================================
7. VOLUME VS OPEN INTEREST
============================================================

VOLUME
The amount of trading activity during the period.

OPEN INTEREST
The total number of outstanding contracts.

The distinction is particularly relevant to futures markets.

VOLUME
Answers:
"How much trading activity occurred?"

OPEN INTEREST
Answers:
"How many contracts remain open?"

The source treats both as secondary indicators that can help assess the
strength of trends and possible trend changes.

============================================================
8. TREND ANALYSIS
============================================================

Trend identification is presented as a central skill.

THREE BASIC MARKET CONDITIONS

UPTREND
Higher peaks and higher troughs.

DOWNTREND
Lower peaks and lower troughs.

TRENDLESS / SIDEWAYS
Price moves without a clear directional structure.

PRACTICAL PRINCIPLE
First determine the overall trend, then interpret smaller time-frame
movements within that context.

The source explicitly emphasizes looking at the overall trend before
interpreting shorter-term price movements.

============================================================
9. SUPPORT AND RESISTANCE
============================================================

SUPPORT
A price zone where buying interest is strong enough to stop or slow a
decline.

RESISTANCE
A price zone where selling interest is strong enough to stop or slow an
advance.

WHY THEY MATTER
Support and resistance can help with:
- Entry decisions
- Exit decisions
- Breakout interpretation
- Stop placement
- Identifying potential turning points

A previously broken support/resistance level can later act in the opposite
role.

KEY IDEA
Do not treat support and resistance as magical exact numbers. The source
frames them as important areas/zones created by market behavior.

============================================================
10. TRENDLINES AND CHANNELS
============================================================

TRENDLINES
Used to represent the direction of a trend.

They are constructed through significant highs or lows.

CHANNELS
A second parallel line can be used to define a channel around the trend.

Typical interpretation:
- Lower boundary can act as support.
- Upper boundary can act as resistance.

A break of a trendline or channel boundary can provide evidence that the
existing movement is changing.

The source also discusses internal trendlines and return/channel lines.

============================================================
11. RETRACEMENTS
============================================================

A retracement is a temporary move against the prevailing trend.

Do not automatically interpret every pullback as a reversal.

The source's basic distinction:

RETRACEMENT
Temporary correction within a larger trend.

REVERSAL
Change in the larger directional movement.

The source's matching material notes a common correction range of roughly
33% to 50% in the classical framework presented.

Use this as a historical technical-analysis reference rather than as a
guaranteed percentage rule.

============================================================
12. TWO-DAY BREAK RULE
============================================================

The source's Q&A describes a two-day rule for validating a trendline break:

For a stronger confirmation of a breakout/breakdown, price should close
above or below the trendline for two successive days.

Purpose:
- Reduce false breakout signals.
- Require additional confirmation before treating the break as decisive.

============================================================
13. PRICE GAPS
============================================================

The source discusses several kinds of gaps.

BREAKAWAY GAP
Associated with a significant move out of a prior formation or trading
area.

MEASURING / RUNAWAY GAP
Occurs within an ongoing trend and can indicate continuation.

EXHAUSTION GAP
Appears near the end of a move and may be associated with trend exhaustion.

ISLAND REVERSAL
A price pattern involving gaps that can mark a reversal structure.

Gaps should be interpreted in context rather than automatically treated as
buy/sell signals.

============================================================
14. REVERSAL DAYS
============================================================

The source discusses reversal days as potential evidence of a change in
market direction.

The Q&A associates them with:
- Significant price movement relative to the previous high/low
- High volume
- A possible shift in market sentiment

Volume is important because it helps judge whether the reversal behavior
has meaningful participation.

============================================================
15. MAJOR REVERSAL PATTERNS
============================================================

The source identifies five common major reversal patterns:

1. HEAD AND SHOULDERS TOP
2. HEAD AND SHOULDERS BOTTOM / INVERSE HEAD AND SHOULDERS
3. DOUBLE TOP
4. DOUBLE BOTTOM
5. V REVERSAL

GENERAL REQUIREMENT
A reversal pattern occurs after a prior trend and implies a possible change
in direction.

============================================================
16. HEAD AND SHOULDERS TOP
============================================================

Structure:
- Left shoulder
- Head (highest peak)
- Right shoulder
- Neckline

Interpretation:
A potential transition from an uptrend to a downtrend.

The source notes that volume commonly behaves differently across the peaks,
with the later peak often showing less volume than the earlier major peak.

CONFIRMATION
A break of the neckline is important evidence.

DO NOT CONFUSE:
A developing pattern is not the same thing as a confirmed reversal.

============================================================
17. INVERSE HEAD AND SHOULDERS
============================================================

The structure is the reverse of the regular head-and-shoulders top.

It can represent a transition from a downtrend toward an uptrend.

Important features:
- Three troughs
- Central trough is the deepest
- Neckline above the pattern
- Break above the neckline provides confirmation

============================================================
18. DOUBLE TOP
============================================================

A double top:
- Usually appears after an uptrend.
- Has two peaks around a similar price level.
- Can signal a bearish reversal.

Confirmation is generally associated with a break below the neckline/support
between the two peaks.

============================================================
19. DOUBLE BOTTOM
============================================================

A double bottom:
- Usually appears after a downtrend.
- Has two troughs around a similar price level.
- Can signal a bullish reversal.

Confirmation occurs when the relevant resistance/neckline is broken.

============================================================
20. V REVERSAL
============================================================

A V reversal is a very rapid transition from a declining move into an
advance, or vice versa.

Because the turn can occur quickly, there may be less time for the
traditional rounded pattern-development process seen in other formations.

============================================================
21. CONTINUATION PATTERNS
============================================================

Continuation patterns suggest the existing trend may resume after a
pause or consolidation.

The source highlights:

- Triangles
- Flags
- Pennants
- Wedges
- Rectangles
- Continuation head-and-shoulders structures

The key distinction:

REVERSAL PATTERN
Change of the prior trend.

CONTINUATION PATTERN
Pause/consolidation before the prior trend resumes.

============================================================
22. TRIANGLES
============================================================

A triangle is generally a period of price compression.

SYMMETRICAL TRIANGLE
- Converging upper and lower trendlines.
- Represents a contracting range.
- Breakout can occur upward or downward.

ASCENDING TRIANGLE
- Upper resistance is relatively flat.
- Lows rise toward that resistance.

DESCENDING TRIANGLE
- Lower support is relatively flat.
- Highs decline toward that support.

The source stresses:
- Volume
- Breakout confirmation
- Measuring implications

============================================================
23. FLAGS AND PENNANTS
============================================================

These are shorter continuation formations that generally follow a prior
sharp move.

FLAG:
A short consolidation that can act as a pause within the existing trend.

PENNANT:
A small converging formation after a strong move.

The prior move is commonly referred to as the flagpole in the terminology
listed by the source.

============================================================
24. WEDGES
============================================================

A wedge is another converging price formation.

Like other patterns, interpretation depends on:
- Prior trend
- Shape
- Volume
- Breakout direction
- Confirmation

============================================================
25. RECTANGLE FORMATION
============================================================

A rectangle represents a trading range where price oscillates between
support and resistance.

A confirmed break of the range can provide a directional signal.

Once a boundary is broken, the role of that level can change.

============================================================
26. BROADENING FORMATIONS
============================================================

A broadening formation is characterized by expanding swings rather than
contracting swings.

The pattern reflects increasing disagreement and volatility.

The source includes broadening formations among the major pattern
terminology but the uploaded summary gives less detail than it does for
head-and-shoulders, double tops/bottoms, and triangles.

============================================================
27. BULL TRAPS
============================================================

A bull trap occurs when price appears to break upward and attract buyers,
but then fails and reverses lower.

Risk:
- Traders may enter on the apparent breakout.
- The move can fail quickly.

Lesson:
Require appropriate confirmation rather than assuming every breakout is
valid.

============================================================
28. CONFIRMATION OF BREAKOUTS
============================================================

Confirmation is recurring throughout the source.

Useful confirming information can include:
- Volume
- Related technical signals
- Pattern completion
- Trendline penetration
- Break of neckline/support/resistance

The source describes confirmation as supporting evidence that increases
confidence that a breakout or reversal is genuine.

============================================================
29. MEASURING TECHNIQUES
============================================================

Technical patterns can be used not only for direction but also for
estimating possible price objectives.

The source discusses:
- Height of patterns
- Measured moves
- Maximum objectives
- Price objectives

General concept:
The size of the completed formation may be used to estimate a potential
post-breakout move.

The source also states that, for some classical reversal structures, the
larger the pattern, the larger the subsequent move may be.

============================================================
30. VOLUME
============================================================

A major theme is:

VOLUME PRECEDES PRICE.

Volume is used as a confirming indicator.

GENERAL INTERPRETATION IN THE SOURCE:
- Rising price + rising volume = stronger confirmation of the move.
- Price movement on weak volume = potentially weaker conviction.
- Strong volume on a breakout = stronger participation.
- Volume behavior around reversals helps evaluate the pattern.

The source repeatedly calls volume an important secondary indicator.

IMPORTANT:
Volume should not be interpreted alone. The PDF's own critical-thinking
section warns against relying exclusively on volume/open-interest metrics.

============================================================
31. OPEN INTEREST
============================================================

Open interest is the number of outstanding futures contracts.

The source uses open interest together with price and volume to interpret
trend strength.

A highlighted relationship is:

DOWNtrend + declining open interest
-> described in the source as a bullish sign.

DOWNtrend + rising open interest
-> described as bearish because new short exposure may be increasing.

When volume rises while open interest falls, the source says this can be
associated with a potential market turn or selling climax because existing
positions may be closing.

============================================================
32. ON-BALANCE VOLUME (OBV)
============================================================

OBV is presented as a cumulative volume indicator designed to show buying
and selling pressure.

GENERAL IDEA:
- Higher-closing periods add volume.
- Lower-closing periods subtract volume.

Interpretation in the source:
Rising OBV can indicate increasing buying pressure.
Declining OBV can indicate increasing selling pressure.

OBV can sometimes change direction before the corresponding price move,
making it useful as an early warning/confirmation tool.

============================================================
33. COMMITMENTS OF TRADERS (COT)
============================================================

The source discusses the use of open-interest positioning in the
Commitments of Traders report.

Participant categories referenced include:
- Commercial hedgers
- Large speculators
- Small traders

The objective is to understand who is holding positions and how market
positioning may influence sentiment.

COT is primarily relevant to futures markets.

============================================================
34. LONG-TERM CHARTS
============================================================

Long-term charts are emphasized as important because they show the larger
context.

The source specifically discusses weekly and monthly charts.

USES:
- Identify major trends.
- Identify major support/resistance.
- See large reversal patterns.
- Understand historical price behavior.
- Put current price action into context.
- Identify longer-term objectives.

Examples of long-term reversal structures include:
- Head and shoulders
- Double tops/bottoms
- Saucers

============================================================
35. LONG-TERM CONTEXT FOR SHORTER-TERM TRADING
============================================================

The source states that long-term charts can improve short-term decisions.

Process:
1. Determine the larger trend.
2. Locate major support/resistance.
3. Review the historical pattern.
4. Then interpret the near-term price movement.

This prevents a short-term chart from being interpreted in isolation.

============================================================
36. MOVING AVERAGES
============================================================

Moving averages are presented as trend-following indicators.

Main purposes:
- Smooth price fluctuations.
- Clarify trend direction.
- Provide timing signals.
- Support crossover methods.

THREE TYPES COVERED:
1. SIMPLE MOVING AVERAGE (SMA)
2. LINEARLY WEIGHTED MOVING AVERAGE (LWMA)
3. EXPONENTIALLY SMOOTHED MOVING AVERAGE (EMA)

============================================================
37. SIMPLE MOVING AVERAGE
============================================================

A simple moving average gives equal weight to each included period.

General formula:

SMA = SUM OF PRICES IN THE PERIOD / NUMBER OF PERIODS

Characteristics:
- Simple to calculate.
- Smoother as the lookback becomes longer.
- Longer averages are less sensitive to short-term price changes.

============================================================
38. WEIGHTED AND EXPONENTIAL AVERAGES
============================================================

LINEARLY WEIGHTED MOVING AVERAGE
Gives greater weight to more recent data.

EXPONENTIAL MOVING AVERAGE
Also places greater emphasis on recent data and produces a smoother
reaction than equal-weighted averages.

The source treats these as alternatives with different sensitivity to price
changes.

============================================================
39. SHORT VS LONG MOVING AVERAGES
============================================================

The source's basic rule:

SHORTER MOVING AVERAGE
- More useful for timing.

LONGER MOVING AVERAGE
- More useful for identifying the broader trend.

Longer averages:
- React more slowly.
- Filter more short-term noise.

Shorter averages:
- React faster.
- Can generate more signals and more noise.

============================================================
40. MOVING-AVERAGE CROSSOVERS
============================================================

A basic crossover approach:

SHORT AVERAGE CROSSES ABOVE LONG AVERAGE
-> Potential bullish signal.

SHORT AVERAGE CROSSES BELOW LONG AVERAGE
-> Potential bearish signal.

The source also describes multiple-average methods.

TRIPLE CROSSOVER
Example given:
- 4-day
- 11-day
- 28-day

A bullish signal is described when the shorter average crosses above the
other averages, indicating stronger trend alignment.

============================================================
41. MOVING AVERAGES WORK BEST IN TRENDS
============================================================

This is an important limitation.

Moving averages are most effective when the market is trending.

In sideways/choppy markets:
- Crossovers can occur frequently.
- Signals can reverse quickly.
- False signals can multiply.

Therefore:

FIRST IDENTIFY MARKET CONDITION.
THEN CHOOSE WHETHER A TREND-FOLLOWING MOVING-AVERAGE METHOD FITS.

============================================================
42. FOUR-WEEK RULE
============================================================

The source discusses the classical four-week rule.

Basic idea:
- A break below the relevant four-week low is used to liquidate longs and
  trigger a short position.
- A break above the relevant four-week high is used to cover shorts and
  establish a long position.

The rule is a systematic trend-following method built around recent price
extremes.

It should be regarded as one historical technique rather than a universal
rule.

============================================================
43. BOLLINGER BANDS
============================================================

The source lists Bollinger Bands among the moving-average-related tools.

Basic concept presented:
- A moving average forms a center.
- Bands are placed around the average based on variability/standard
  deviation.

Uses described:
- Assess volatility.
- Help evaluate overbought/oversold conditions.
- Support interpretation of moving-average signals.

The source's Q&A says band expansion/divergence can accompany a new trend
while convergence can occur during continuation/consolidation.

============================================================
44. OSCILLATORS
============================================================

Oscillators are used mainly to evaluate momentum and shorter-term extremes.

The source includes:
- RSI
- MACD
- Stochastic-related K%D
- %R
- Rate of Change (ROC)
- Commodity Channel Index (CCI)
- Momentum

USES:
- Identify overbought conditions.
- Identify oversold conditions.
- Identify momentum shifts.
- Detect divergence.
- Detect failure swings.
- Provide additional evidence near market extremes.

============================================================
45. RSI
============================================================

The source gives the conventional 0-100 RSI scale.

Common reference levels in the PDF:

RSI ABOVE 70
-> Traditionally considered overbought.

RSI BELOW 30
-> Traditionally considered oversold.

IMPORTANT:
"Overbought" does not automatically mean "sell."
"Oversold" does not automatically mean "buy."

Context and trend matter.

============================================================
46. DIVERGENCE
============================================================

Divergence occurs when price and an oscillator fail to confirm each other.

BEARISH DIVERGENCE
Price makes a higher high, but the oscillator makes a lower high or fails
to confirm the new high.

Interpretation:
Momentum may be weakening.

BULLISH DIVERGENCE
Price makes a lower low, but the oscillator makes a higher low or otherwise
fails to confirm the weakness.

Interpretation:
Downward momentum may be weakening.

Divergence is a warning signal, not a guarantee of reversal.

============================================================
47. FAILURE SWINGS IN OSCILLATORS
============================================================

A failure swing occurs when an oscillator forms a structure suggesting that
the existing momentum is weakening without a corresponding continuation
in price.

The source treats this as a possible reversal warning.

============================================================
48. ZERO LINE
============================================================

The zero line is a reference point for momentum oscillators.

Crossing above or below the zero line can signal a change in momentum
direction.

The source presents this as an additional signal rather than a standalone
system.

============================================================
49. OSCILLATORS AND TREND CONTEXT
============================================================

One of the most important cautions in the source:

Oscillators can be especially useful near the end of market moves, but they
can produce misleading signals when used without context.

In strong trends:
- An oscillator may remain overbought for a long time.
- It may remain oversold for a long time.
- A trader who automatically fades every extreme can fight the trend.

Therefore:

TREND FIRST
OSCILLATOR SECOND

Use oscillator information to refine interpretation rather than allowing
it to automatically override the larger market structure.

============================================================
50. CONTRARY OPINION
============================================================

Contrary Opinion attempts to interpret extreme crowd sentiment.

The source defines a true contrarian as someone who acts opposite to the
majority.

A very high bullish-consensus reading can be viewed as a warning that the
market may be near a top.

The source gives a historical example of bullish consensus above about
75% as a potential danger zone.

IMPORTANT:
Contrary opinion does not mean:
"Always do the opposite of everyone."

It means:
Look for extremes in sentiment and assess whether the consensus has become
excessive.

============================================================
51. POINT AND FIGURE CHARTING
============================================================

Point and figure charts:
- Focus on price rather than time.
- Use X columns and O columns.
- Suppress much of the time-based noise.
- Can provide specific entry and exit points.
- Can highlight support and resistance.

KEY ADVANTAGE:
The trader can concentrate on price movement without every small time-based
fluctuation appearing on the chart.

============================================================
52. BOX SIZE IN POINT AND FIGURE
============================================================

Box size determines chart sensitivity.

SMALLER BOX:
- More detailed.
- More sensitive to price changes.
- More signals/noise.

LARGER BOX:
- More simplified.
- Filters more minor price movement.
- May omit smaller changes.

The appropriate setting depends on the purpose of the chart.

============================================================
53. REVERSAL CRITERION IN POINT AND FIGURE
============================================================

The reversal criterion defines the amount of opposing price movement
required before the chart changes column/direction.

Its purpose is to prevent every small counter-move from being treated as a
new trend.

The exact reversal rule depends on the point-and-figure construction being
used.

============================================================
54. JAPANESE CANDLESTICKS
============================================================

The source introduces Japanese candlestick charting as a way to visualize
price action.

CANDLE COMPONENTS:
- Real body
- Upper shadow
- Lower shadow

BODY:
The distance between open and close.

SHADOWS / WICKS:
Show the high and low extremes during the period.

The source lists:
- Doji
- Spinning tops
- Morning Star
- Evening Star
- Dark Cloud Cover
- Piercing Line
- Rising Three Methods

============================================================
55. DOJI
============================================================

A Doji has little difference between the open and close.

Interpretation:
- Indecision
- Balance between buyers and sellers
- Potential warning when it appears at an important market extreme

A Doji is not automatically a reversal.

Context is essential.

============================================================
56. SPINNING TOP
============================================================

A spinning top is another indecision-type candle.

It typically has:
- A relatively small body.
- Upper and lower shadows.
- Evidence that buyers and sellers have both moved price but neither
  has clearly maintained control.

Its significance depends on where it appears in the larger trend.

============================================================
57. LONG DAY / SHORT DAY
============================================================

LONG DAY
Large difference between open and close.

SHORT DAY
Small difference between open and close.

The size of the body gives information about the strength of movement in
that period.

============================================================
58. MORNING STAR / EVENING STAR
============================================================

MORNING STAR
A bullish reversal-type candlestick formation.

EVENING STAR
A bearish reversal-type candlestick formation.

The source includes them as examples of multi-candle reversal patterns.

They should be evaluated with the preceding trend and other evidence rather
than used as isolated automatic signals.

============================================================
59. DARK CLOUD COVER / PIERCING LINE
============================================================

DARK CLOUD COVER
A bearish candlestick reversal formation.

PIERCING LINE
A bullish candlestick reversal formation.

Again, the important idea is context:
- Existing trend
- Location
- Confirmation
- Volume
- Subsequent price action

============================================================
60. CANDLESTICKS = PRICE BEHAVIOR
============================================================

A candlestick contains more information than simply whether price closed
up or down.

It also shows:
- Where the market opened.
- How far buyers pushed price.
- How far sellers pushed price.
- Where price finally closed.

This makes candlesticks useful for studying short-term market psychology.

62. PRICE PATTERN CHECKLIST
============================================================

Before acting on a chart pattern, ask:

1. What is the prior trend?
2. Is this potentially a reversal or continuation structure?
3. Has the pattern actually completed?
4. Where is the breakout/breakdown level?
5. What is the nearest support/resistance?
6. Is volume confirming the move?
7. Is there a divergence?
8. Is the broader time frame aligned?
9. What is the estimated price objective?
10. What would invalidate the pattern?

68. MULTI-TIME-FRAME PROCESS
============================================================

The source strongly supports using longer-term context before shorter-term
interpretation.

Suggested source-derived process:

1. Start with a longer-range chart.
2. Identify the major trend.
3. Mark major support and resistance.
4. Identify major patterns.
5. Move to the intermediate chart.
6. Identify the current trend/correction.
7. Move to the shorter time frame.
8. Use timing tools such as trendlines, moving averages, oscillators,
   or candlesticks.
9. Do not let a very short-term signal contradict a clearly established
   larger trend without additional evidence.

69. WHAT THE SOURCE SAYS ABOUT "CONFIRMATION"
============================================================

Confirmation is one of the most repeated ideas.

Confirmation can come from:
- Another market average
- Volume
- Open interest
- Break of a neckline
- Break of support/resistance
- Multiple technical indicators
- Price continuing to hold above/below a breakout area
- Multi-time-frame agreement

General principle:

ONE SIGNAL = OBSERVATION
MULTIPLE SUPPORTING SIGNALS = STRONGER EVIDENCE

This does not mean adding dozens of indicators.
The indicators should provide genuinely different information.

70. IMPORTANT CAUTIONS FROM THE PDF ITSELF
============================================================

The uploaded summary contains critical-thinking sections that explicitly
warn against treating technical tools as infallible.

A. PATTERN CAUTION
Reversal and continuation patterns can fail.
Do not rely on pattern recognition alone.

B. VOLUME / OPEN-INTEREST CAUTION
These are useful secondary indicators but should not be interpreted in
isolation.

C. OSCILLATOR CAUTION
RSI/MACD-type tools may give false signals in trending markets.

D. CONTRARY-OPINION CAUTION
Extreme sentiment does not guarantee an immediate reversal.

E. CONTEXT MATTERS
Broader market conditions and other influencing variables matter.

This caution is important because the book's educational framework is
strongest when the tools are combined with context rather than used as
automatic buy/sell machines.

STEP 1 — LONG-TERM CONTEXT
Identify the major trend on a weekly/monthly or other longer-range chart.

STEP 2 — MARKET STRUCTURE
Mark major support, resistance, channels, and important highs/lows.

STEP 6 — CHECK OPEN INTEREST WHERE RELEVANT
Especially in futures.

STEP 7 — CHECK MOMENTUM
Use oscillators and divergence as secondary evidence.

STEP 8 — CHECK MOVING AVERAGES
Use them for trend-following/timing where market conditions are suitable.

- Entry
- Invalidation
- Support/resistance
- Potential objective

Avoid acting solely on an unconfirmed pattern.

STEP 12 — REVIEW
Record whether the setup actually behaved as expected.

72. KEY FORMULAS / MEASURES TO REMEMBER
============================================================

SIMPLE MOVING AVERAGE

SMA = (P1 + P2 + ... + Pn) / n

Where:
P = price for each included period
n = number of periods

MOMENTUM
The source describes momentum as the difference between the current
price and the price a fixed number of periods earlier.

RATE OF CHANGE
A normalized form of price change over a fixed time interval.

PATTERN MEASUREMENT
Many chart formations use the height/width of the pattern to estimate a
possible post-breakout price objective.

73. DEFINITIONS — QUICK MEMORY SECTION
============================================================

TREND
Directional pattern of peaks and troughs.

SUPPORT
Area where buying pressure tends to slow or stop a decline.

RESISTANCE
Area where selling pressure tends to slow or stop an advance.

RETRACEMENT
Temporary counter-trend movement.

REVERSAL
Change in the larger trend.

CONFIRMATION
Additional evidence supporting a signal.

DIVERGENCE
Price and indicator fail to confirm one another.

VOLUME
Trading activity.

OPEN INTEREST
Outstanding futures contracts.

MOVING AVERAGE
Smoothed price measure used primarily for trend and timing.

OSCILLATOR
Momentum indicator that helps identify extremes and divergences.

POINT AND FIGURE
Price-focused chart that largely removes the time dimension.

CANDLESTICK
Visual representation of open, high, low, and close.

        ->

        ->
